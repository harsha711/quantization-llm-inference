## Invalid: E050 4096² cyclic cells (found 2026-11-20)

Four 4096² matrices (4 × 8.65 MB = 34.6 MB) are about the 32 MiB L2, so E050's four-matrix cycle does not force DRAM reads at that shape. Check (calculation): DRAM floor at E057's measured 427 GB/s is 20.4 µs (M4) / 20.9 µs (M16); E050 measured Marlin 15.1–16.7 / 19.7–22.4 µs and `tc` 18.3–19.3 / 20.4–21.4 µs: every M4 time is BELOW its floor, so partly L2-served; at M16 three times are slightly above the 20.9 µs floor (Marlin s1 22.39, `tc` s2 21.39, `tc_ladder` s2 21.25), the rest at or below it (corrected 2026-11-20: earlier text said all were below). The working set alone settles it: 34,603,008 B = 1.03× the 33,554,432 B L2. M64/M128 lie above the floor only because they are compute-bound; the same partial caching cannot be excluded. Therefore every E050 4096² absolute time and fraction of Marlin (incl. "0.89–0.94", "1.10 at M16", the 10–13% Marlin swing) is not a DRAM-regime measurement; the swing is plausibly this (hypothesis, untested). E050's acceptance at 4096² (tc_ladder/tc) compared two of our kernels under the same partial caching: report it only with this caveat. Rectangles (4 × 23.2 MB ≈ 93 MB) are unaffected: every rectangle time is above its floor. A valid 4096² cyclic comparison needs ≥ 8 matrices (≈ 69 MB): done in E058 (above); E058's 4-matrix control reproduces the cache-served M4 values on fresh seeds. Finding first raised by a teammate's analysis. Old records stay unchanged.

Rule: before quoting any timing, compare it with the DRAM floor (modelled bytes ÷ 427 GB/s) and check the rotating working set against the 32 MiB L2. Floor statuses (v2 after review): BELOW_STRICT = below weights(+scales) ÷ 448 GB/s spec and FLOP ÷ 56.95 TFLOP/s at the 3090 MHz max clock, physically impossible from DRAM; BELOW_MEASURED = below the full byte model ÷ 427.2 GB/s (best measured, not a physical ceiling), almost certainly cache-served; WS_SMALL = DRAM regime not confirmed (2× L2 is a heuristic margin); OK = mostly DRAM (E049 saw 4–6% L2 re-reads even at 2.8× L2); OK_FLUSHED = store flush, eviction not proven and flush write-back can inflate times. Tool: `python3 scripts/checks/floorcheck.py` (tests: `python3 -m unittest scripts/checks/test_floorcheck.py`) flags rows of E050 and E051 a/b/c (including unfused and dequant byte models); extend it (tests first) before quoting any other experiment's timings.

## Errata from the independent audit (2026-11-20)

- Words: a cut in time is not "% faster". 45% less decode time = 1.83× speed; 25% = 1.33×; `tc_ladder`/`tc` 0.826–0.872 = 13–17% less time = 1.15–1.21×. Say "X% less time" or give the speed ratio.
- Decode vs Marlin in the model is "comparable" (E052/E054). Never "faster", "wins", "in our favour".
- The context rise of decode time is attributed to attention only up to 2048 (E056: attention + copies +773 µs/step × 63 ≈ 49 ms of the 54 ms rise); beyond 8192 it is unprofiled (E029).
- E057: the copy-run clock printed by `analysis.txt` (SM 180 MHz, mem 405 MHz) is a lagging sampler, not the copy clock; never quote it. CPU cycles:u ÷ task-clock gives 4.139 GHz, 0.6% above lscpu's 4115.8 MHz max: unexplained (observation).

- E054 ran on 10 nodes and 5 GPU MODELS (RTX 5060 Ti ×5, RTX 4060 Ti ×2 = c20 8 GB + c55 16 GB, RTX A6000, A100 80GB PCIe, RTX 3060 Ti); "6 GPU types" in AGENTS/EXPERIMENTS counted the two 4060 Ti memory sizes separately. Say "5 GPU models (10 nodes)".
